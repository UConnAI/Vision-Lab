How the mask maps back to the picture

At every complete 3 × 3 window, multiply the nine grayscale brightness values by the nine weights and add. The signed sum is the filter response. We use its absolute value so both dark-to-light and light-to-dark boundaries can appear.

The response map is two pixels smaller in each dimension because there is no padding. Output (row, column) maps to the center pixel (row + 1, column + 1) in the original working image. Border pixels have no output. The mask keeps responses whose strength reaches your threshold; those positions are painted purple over the photo.

The response-map colors are contrast enhanced to make weak edges visible; the mask threshold uses the unenhanced strength. Strength = |sum of pixel × weight| / sum of |weight|. This keeps the threshold scale stable when weights change. It is a contrast measure, not a probability or an object label. An outline filter can respond to parts of a circle, but does not recognize a circle.

What the score means

For each complete 4 × 4 patch, subtract the mean brightness, multiply by the centered kernel weights, and add the products. Divide by the contrast lengths of the kernel and patch to get a normalized pattern score. A flat zero kernel scores 0. Neighboring peaks are merged before checking the two eye regions.

The contribution above shows the centered product for your selected weight. A dark pixel and a negative centered weight produce a positive contribution. The same kernel slides across every patch, so one weight is not permanently attached to one photo pixel.
